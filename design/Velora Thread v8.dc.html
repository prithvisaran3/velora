It is one 3D object, it is permanent brand furniture, and it has no idea how many sarees you sell. Six or six hundred — the thread does not care.

WHAT THE THREAD IS
GEOMETRY
A three.js TubeGeometry along a live CatmullRom curve, radius 1.4px, 9 control points. Metallic gold, roughness .22, one warm key light. That is the whole object — under 40 KB.
IT REACTS TO YOU
Cursor bends the nearest control points with spring damping (stiffness .06, damping .82). Move fast and it whips; hold still and it settles. On a product card it tightens into the card's frame.
IT IS NOT DECORATION
The same thread is the loading bar, the page-transition stitch, the underline on links, the checkout progress and the tracker. One object doing seven jobs is why it feels designed rather than sprinkled.
ZERO PRODUCT COUPLING
No saree data reaches it. Ships with the build, identical forever.
Preview stitches on load. In the build it also tracks the pointer continuously.

NO NUMBERS ANYWHERE
"All sarees", not "Twelve sarees". No "48 a month", no "12 pieces", no edit counter. Nothing in the copy breaks when she adds stock.
GRID, NOT A DECK
4-up desktop, 2-up mobile, LOAD MORE at the end. Works identically at 6 items and 600. Filters do the narrowing that a curated count used to imply.
THE STITCH
Each card's gold frame draws itself on intersection, 150ms apart. That is the thread arriving — and it is the only motion in the grid, so the photographs stay the loudest thing on screen.

WHY CONES, NOT DOTS
IT IS THE WEAVER'S SHELF
Every Erode weaving shed has a wall of thread cones. It is the most recognisable object in the trade after the loom, and it makes a colour filter feel like a place instead of a control.
3D, CHEAP
Seven instanced cone meshes, one material, vertex-coloured. Hover spins the cone and unspools a short thread toward the cursor. Click and that thread whips across and stitches the filtered grid.
FIXED SET
Seven colours, hard-coded. Products map onto them by tag. She never adds a cone — she just tags a saree.
This replaces the flat swatch row. Same function, far more memorable.

WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT ANSWER FOR AN ECOMMERCE SITE
An unfolding saree is a one-joke animation — impressive once, tedious by the third visit, and it competes with the product. A thread that builds the interface gets more interesting the longer you shop, because it is doing real work: loading, filtering, progressing, confirming. It is also the cheapest 3D you will ever ship — one tube, one material — so it stays fast on the Android phones your customers actually use.
WHAT I DROPPED FROM V5
The pile, the counter, the twelve, the snap, the deck, every hardcoded number. Products are now a plain filterable grid that behaves the same at any size, and the only motion touching them is the gold frame drawing itself.

What survived: kora beige, saffron, marigold, the warm brown shadow rule, the type, and the product page layout you liked.
