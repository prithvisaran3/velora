A living gold thread, always moving.
The threads no longer stitch once and stop. They breathe continuously — six filaments drifting on long, offset cycles, with light running along each one at its own speed. That travelling glint is what makes it read as spun gold rather than a drawn line.

The saree itself is never animated. It is only ever a still photograph.
And the thread takes the colour of the room. Gold at home; open a maroon saree and every filament bleeds to maroon over 900ms, then returns to gold when you leave.

It still has no idea how many sarees you sell. Six or six hundred — the thread does not care.

WHY IT LOOKS LIKE SILK
THREE LAYERS PER THREAD
A dull base strand, a bright filament with light running along it, and a rare white specular that passes every few seconds. One strand looks like a line; three looks like twisted silk. This is the entire trick.
IT REACTS TO YOU
Cursor bends the nearest control points with spring damping (stiffness .06, damping .82). Move fast and it whips; hold still and it settles. On a product card it tightens into the card's frame.
NEVER STILL, NEVER BUSY
Six threads on 15–29s drift cycles, all prime-ish so they never sync. Motion is always present and never demands attention — the page feels alive when you are reading, not distracting.
ZERO PRODUCT COUPLING
No saree data reaches it. Ships with the build, identical forever.
Everything above runs forever. In the build the threads also bend toward your cursor.

HOW THE COLOUR WORKS
ONE CSS VARIABLE
Every thread reads --thread and --thread-lit. The product page sets them from the saree's tag; a 900ms transition does the rest. Nothing re-renders, nothing reloads.
THE PAGE FOLLOWS TOO
The ground shifts 4% toward the same hue — beige stays beige, but a maroon room feels warmer and a peacock room cooler. Subtle enough that nobody names it.
GOLD IS HOME
Leaving a product returns everything to zari over the same 900ms. Gold is the brand; the other colours are visits.
NEVER ON THE PHOTO
The tint touches threads and ground only. Her photographs are never colour-shifted — that would be lying about the product.
Top strip cycles all four so you can see the transition. Live, it changes only on navigation.

WHY CONES, NOT DOTS
IT IS THE WEAVER'S SHELF
Every Erode weaving shed has a wall of thread cones. It is the most recognisable object in the trade after the loom, and it makes a colour filter feel like a place instead of a control.
3D, CHEAP
Seven instanced cone meshes, one material, vertex-coloured. Hover spins the cone and unspools a short thread toward the cursor. Click and that thread whips across and stitches the filtered grid.
FIXED SET
Seven colours, hard-coded. Products map onto them by tag. She never adds a cone — she just tags a saree.
This replaces the flat swatch row. Same function, far more memorable.

WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT ANSWER FOR AN ECOMMERCE SITE
An unfolding saree is a one-joke animation — impressive once, tedious by the third visit, and it competes with the product. A thread that builds the interface gets more interesting the longer you shop, because it is doing real work: loading, filtering, progressing, confirming. It is also the cheapest 3D you will ever ship — one tube, one material — so it stays fast on the Android phones your customers actually use.
WHAT I DROPPED FROM V5
The pile, the counter, the twelve, the snap, the deck, every hardcoded number. Products are now a plain filterable grid that behaves the same at any size, and the only motion touching them is the gold frame drawing itself.

What survived: kora beige, saffron, marigold, the warm brown shadow rule, the type, and the product page layout you liked.
